# P3 → P4 · secretary cabinet study

The existing portrait topic cabinet leads into an integrated fold-down writing desk, then into the previously approved, text-free mobile folio. This is an isolated motion study, not a change to the production P1–P4 design.

## Storyboard

- 0–29%: establish the full cabinet and its lower secretary compartment from the original one-bay browsing scale.
- 25–67%: lower the front panel around two fixed lower hinges. The outer wood panel is visible until it turns edge-on; the inner writing surface and folio then become visible. Two constant-length articulated stays connect cabinet and panel.
- 70–100%: approach and deproject the same folio illustration into the approved mobile reading composition. No topical image is transferred into the folio. The folio remains the collection-wide secondary-style index.

The cabinet, writing surface, and page are separate layers. Planar homographies map the existing pen artwork through the movement. Topic images retain `object-fit: contain` and all six original source exports.

## Desktop version

Wide viewports (at least 900px wide, aspect ratio at least 1.1) use the full browser width. The initial shot shows approximately three portrait cabinet bays; the lower secretary compartment receives a separate closer camera framing. The opening mechanism and illustrated layers are shared with mobile.

The final folio uses `book-desktop-approved-v1.png`, the previously approved 1672×941 blank double-page illustration (`exec-2ed82d85-5222-42a2-b0bb-c3e5171b8710.png`). A viewport-shaped ivory canvas contains the entire illustration at its native aspect ratio, including on ultrawide screens. That same canvas is projected onto the writing surface and approached continuously. There is no late image swap.

Desktop includes previous/next topic buttons, keyboard navigation, native trackpad scrolling, and mouse dragging with scale-correct coordinates and snap on release. Vertical wheel scrolling continues to advance the scene normally. Resizing retains progress and the current cabinet position. Narrow/portrait layouts retain the accepted mobile composition.
